# The DSA experiment: patterns over memorization

I spend most of my day building data pipelines — writing SQL, working with bigquery, Apache Airflow and Google Cloud. Those are the skills my job actually rewards. Interviews measure something adjacent: solving a narrow problem, under time pressure, with the thought process visible.

In my earlier post I wrote about why a data engineer would spend evenings on data structures and algorithms. This is the follow-up — about *how* I'm learning, and what I changed when the obvious approach stopped working.

## What didn't work

My first instinct was to open a LeetCode problem and immediately look for the solution. It felt faster, but it was teaching me nothing: every new problem still felt completely new. Memorizing solutions didn't generalise. Nothing I copied stuck.

## What I changed

I replaced "read the answer" with a fixed five-step process:

1. Understand the problem.
2. Solve it manually using a small example.
3. Identify the underlying pattern.
4. Write the optimized solution.
5. Analyze the time and space complexity.

Step two is the one I never used to do. Doing it forces the pattern out before any code exists.

## Patterns over memorization

The real insight so far is that DSA is mostly pattern recognition. Once I can name the pattern a problem is using, the implementation follows. The families I'm working through: arrays, two pointers, sliding window, binary search, dynamic programming, trees, graphs, hash maps.

## The measure I'm using

I'm not trying to solve hundreds of problems as quickly as possible. The goal I set myself is deliberately the opposite: go deep enough on each problem that I can confidently explain my thought process when it matters. Quality of learning beats problem count.

## What I'll write next

Over the coming weeks I'll keep documenting this the way I have been: DSA concepts explained in simple language, LeetCode solutions with detailed explanations, Python tips for interview preparation, SQL interview questions, and data engineering interview notes.

Whether patterns-over-memorization survives contact with a real interview is the open question. That is the experiment.
